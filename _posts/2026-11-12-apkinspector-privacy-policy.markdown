**Information Collection and Use**

APK Inspector is a developer tool that shows the manifest, signing certificates, components and native libraries of the apps installed on your device and of APK files you open, and checks whether their native libraries are compatible with 16 KB memory pages.

*   **Installed apps are analyzed only on your device.** To do this, the app reads the list of installed apps, their package information (such as version, SDK levels, install source and install time), their signing certificates, and their APK files (the manifest, components and native libraries).
*   **APK files you open or share with the app are analyzed only on your device.** The app reads only the file you pick or share. It copies the file to its private cache because the analysis needs random access to the file, and deletes the copy as soon as the analysis finishes or is cancelled. Leftover copies (for example, if the app was closed during an analysis) are deleted the next time the app starts.
*   **Analysis results are kept only in memory** (the most recent results) and are not saved to storage.
*   **None of this information is sent off your device.** The names, package names, files, certificates and analysis results of the apps you inspect are never uploaded to me, to Firebase, to advertising partners, or to any other server, and they are not used for advertising.
*   **Use of the QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission.** The app uses the `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES` permission only to see every app installed on your device, which is the core function of the app (inspecting installed apps). Without it, Android would hide most installed apps from the list.
*   The app does not request storage permission. Apart from the notification permission on Android 13 and later (used only to show notices about the app, and optional), the app does not ask you for any additional permissions, and it does not collect your name, email address, contacts, location, or other files.
*   Your settings (for example, dark mode) are stored only on your device.

The app does use third-party services that may collect information used to identify you. These services are used for advertising, usage analytics and crash reporting, and they do not receive the list of your installed apps, the files you open, or the identity of the apps you inspect.

*   **AdMob (Google) and AppLovin (ad mediation):** may collect the advertising ID, device information (such as model and OS version), IP address and approximate location derived from it, and ad interaction data, to show and measure ads. In regions where consent is required (such as the EEA and the UK), the app asks for your consent through Google's consent form before requesting ads, and ads are not requested without it.
*   **Google Analytics for Firebase:** collects app usage events (such as app opens, screens viewed and session length), app instance ID, and device information, to understand how the app is used. The app also sends anonymous events about how its features are used: how an analysis was started (installed app, opened file or shared file), how long it took, the 16 KB result category, the number of warnings, which tab was viewed, and whether an opened file could be read. These events never include the name, package name or contents of the app or file you inspect.
*   **Firebase Crashlytics:** collects crash logs and diagnostic data (such as the error type, stack trace, device model, OS version and app state at the time of the crash), to fix errors. Error reports do not include the name or package name of the app you inspect.
*   **Firebase Cloud Messaging and Firebase In-App Messaging:** use a Firebase installation ID to deliver notices about the app.
*   **Firebase Cloud Firestore, Cloud Storage and App Check:** load the list of my other apps (names, descriptions and icons). App Check uses Google Play Integrity to check that these requests come from a genuine copy of the app.
*   **Firebase Remote Config and A/B Testing:** use a Firebase installation ID to load app settings (for example, how ads are laid out) and to compare different versions of those settings across groups of users.
*   **Google Play Age Signals:** the app asks Google Play whether your Google account is supervised (for example, a child's account managed by a parent). If it is, the app asks AdMob to treat its ad requests as directed to children and to show only ads suitable for all ages. The app does not store this information or send it anywhere else.
